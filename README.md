# Dual inoculation with rhizobia and arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi has synergistic effects on legume biomass under elevated carbon dioxide
Legumes depend on symbiotic associations with arbuscular mycorrhizal (AM) fungi and nitrogen-fixing rhizobia, yet how these interactions respond to rising atmospheric carbon dioxide remains poorly understood. Because both mutualists exchange limiting nutrients for plant-derived carbon, elevated carbon dioxide may shift the balance of resource exchange and influence the outcomes of tripartite symbioses. We aimed to determine how elevated carbon dioxide affects plant growth, nutrient uptake, and microbial colonization in the prairie legume Lespedeza capitata when paired with AM fungi, rhizobia, or both.
